# Customer Research Skill

## Goal
Turn accessible evidence into a transparent summary of audience needs, objections, alternatives, and testable hypotheses without pretending interviews or surveys occurred when they did not.

## Workflow
1. Define the question, decision, audience/market, and what evidence could change the decision.
2. Prefer primary sources: official product pages, public documentation, public reviews, public community discussions, published studies, and user-provided materials. Record URLs, dates when available, and access limitations.
3. Collect only public, permitted evidence. Respect platform rules, robots, rate limits, privacy, and access controls. Do not scrape private communities, personal data, or gated content.
4. Quote only wording actually visible in the source; preserve wording and link the source. Otherwise paraphrase and label it. Never fabricate customer statements or generalize from a biased sample.
5. Group evidence into needs, triggers, outcomes, objections, alternatives, buying criteria, and vocabulary. Track source count and sample limitations.
6. For each theme, state supporting sources, counterevidence, confidence, and unknowns. Avoid false precision and invented market sizing.
7. Translate findings into audience profiles, messaging implications, product questions, and low-cost tests.
8. Verify cited sources, remove duplicate evidence, check quote accuracy, and disclose coverage gaps.

## Output
- Research question and scope
- Sources reviewed (URL, type, date when available)
- Key themes with evidence and confidence
- Audience needs, objections, and language
- Counterevidence and limitations
- Testable hypotheses and low-cost next steps
- Claims not yet safe to use in marketing

## Safety and cost
Use public or user-authorized sources and existing free tools. No paid research APIs, fake interviews, fake survey responses, fabricated quotations, or unsolicited contact. Avoid unnecessary personal data; summarize patterns rather than profiling identifiable individuals.
