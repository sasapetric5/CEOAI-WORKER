# Copywriting Skill

## Goal
Create clear, persuasive copy matching the audience's real problem and the product's verified capabilities. Clarity and trust outrank hype.

## Workflow
1. Identify page type, target audience, desired action, language, tone, constraints, and existing copy. Ask one focused question only when a missing fact blocks useful work.
2. Build a fact sheet separating verified product facts, user-provided claims, hypotheses, and unknowns. Never invent customer counts, testimonials, certifications, savings, performance metrics, integrations, guarantees, or case studies.
3. Clarify who the product helps, the problem solved, how it works, and what differentiates it only where supported by evidence.
4. Draft for the page's purpose: specific headline, explanatory subheadline, concrete benefits, credible proof, clear CTA, and relevant objection handling.
5. Prefer concrete language. Explain material limitations. Do not imply an agent performed work when it only generated a template or recommendation.
6. When useful, provide three meaningfully different headline/CTA options with rationale and trade-offs.
7. Review factual accuracy, clarity, audience fit, specificity, tone, accessibility, jargon, and support for every claim.
8. Deliver a draft and a checklist of facts requiring owner approval.

## Output
- Audience and conversion goal
- Verified facts and unknowns
- Recommended draft
- Optional alternative headlines and CTAs
- Claims requiring confirmation
- QA checklist

## Safety and cost
No paid copy tools or APIs. No fake testimonials, reviews, urgency, deceptive scarcity, hidden terms, or unsupported outcome guarantees. Do not publish, send, or deploy copy; provide a draft for review.
